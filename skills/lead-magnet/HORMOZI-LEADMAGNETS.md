# Hormozi's lead-magnet framework ($100M Leads)

The reasoning spine. Cite the framework by name in the spec so the reader sees the logic.

## What a lead magnet actually is

> "A lead magnet is a complete solution to a narrow problem."

It is not a teaser. You **fully solve** one small problem — and solving it well does two things: it proves you can solve the *big* problem (competence + trust via reciprocity), and it **reveals the next problem**, which only the core offer solves. That revealed gap is the engine that converts a free lead into a buyer.

Why use one at all: free + valuable gets far more people to raise their hand than a cold pitch, and — when tied tightly to the core offer — the hands that go up are the *right* ones. A magnet misaligned with the offer attracts freebie-seekers and is worse than none.

## The 7 steps to build one

1. **Identify the narrow problem.** Pick one real, urgent problem your ICP has, whose solution naturally exposes the bigger problem your core offer solves. Narrow > broad — specificity is what makes it feel made-for-me.

2. **Pick how you'll solve it — the type.** Three archetypes:
   - **Reveal a problem (diagnose).** Show them a problem they didn't know they had / didn't know the size of. Audits, assessments, quizzes, calculators, scans, teardowns, benchmark reports. *Great when the prospect underestimates the pain.*
   - **Sample / free trial.** A real taste of the core offer — one free unit of the thing. *Great when the offer is self-evidently good once experienced.*
   - **One step of a multi-step process.** Give one complete step of the larger journey; the paid offer does the rest. A template, a single done-for-you piece, a starter kit. *Great when the full process is obviously bigger than one step.*

3. **Pick the format / delivery.** Software/tool · Information (checklist, guide, mini-course, webinar, swipe file, report) · Service (free audit/consult/done-for-you sample) · Physical thing. Choose the cheapest-to-deliver format that still lands a *real* win at high perceived value. Tools and templates scale; live services don't but convert hard.

4. **Make the offer for it irresistible & name it.** The name must make the value obvious and specific — what they get + for whom + the result/speed. Avoid vague ("Free Guide"); prefer concrete ("The 5-Point Support-Cost Audit: find $X in savings in 20 min"). Test several names.

5. **Balance value vs. your cost.** Make perceived value high enough that they'd *pay* for it, while keeping your cost to deliver low — productize/automate so it scales. "Give until they ask."

6. **Make it easy to consume → fast win.** Remove friction and time-to-value. The quick win is what triggers reciprocity and belief. If it takes work or wait to get value, it fails.

7. **Tell them the next step.** Give a **reason why** it's free (keeps it credible), and end every magnet with a clear CTA into the core offer. The hand-off should feel like the obvious next move *because* the magnet revealed the next problem.

## The bridge (the part people miss)

Map it explicitly: **narrow problem solved → win delivered → next problem revealed → core offer is the solution → CTA.** If you can't draw that line, the magnet generates leads that don't convert. The magnet and the offer must be two links of one chain.

## Quick scoring checklist

For each candidate, rate 1–5: narrow & complete · reveals next problem · quick win · worth paying for · ICP-aligned (right crowd) · cheap to deliver · clear next step. Pick the highest total — but a hard fail on *"reveals next problem"* or *"ICP-aligned"* disqualifies regardless of total.
