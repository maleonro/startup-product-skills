# ICP Definition

First skill of the GTM set (`icp-definition` → `lead-magnet` → `gtm-roadmap`). Goal: turn "we sell X to everyone" into a sharp, evidence-backed ICP that you can act on — **anchored on who you can genuinely help most**, not who's easiest to spam.

Principle to honor throughout: the win condition is the prospect thinking *"thank god you reached out"*, not reach volume. An ICP that produces irrelevant outreach is a failed ICP.

It works the same whether you run it on your own startup or for a client (consultant, agency, fractional GTM). "The company" below means whoever owns the offer.

## Approach

**Pull live data first, then interview to complete and align.** Don't ask questions you can answer from public data — show what you found and let the owner correct it. The frameworks come from Hormozi; see [HORMOZI.md](HORMOZI.md).

## Workflow

1. **Get the seed.** You need at minimum: the company + its LinkedIn/website, and **2–5 of its *best* existing customers** — best = most profitable, happiest, fastest to result, lowest effort to serve. With no customers yet, use the offer + 2–5 aspirational target accounts. Ask only for this.

2. **Pull live data.** Reverse-engineer the best customers and scan the company's own footprint with whatever LinkedIn / company-data tools you have (a LinkedIn MCP, Sales Navigator, Apollo, Clay, web search). Find what the best customers *have in common* — industry, size, buyer role, stage, tech, recent events. See [SIGNALS.md](SIGNALS.md) for the signal → data-source map. Don't boil the ocean: a handful of enriched profiles/companies beats a giant search.

3. **Reconcile the positioning.** Cross-check the *stated* positioning across surfaces — founder's LinkedIn headline, profile summary, company tagline, website. They often contradict: a horizontal headline ("we help [broad audience]") vs. a vertical site ("the backbone of [one industry]"). **Surface every contradiction explicitly and make the owner resolve it** — vertical-vs-horizontal changes the whole ICP, so this is usually the highest-leverage moment. The ICP can't be sharper than the positioning it rests on.

4. **Score the market (Hormozi).** Pressure-test the emerging segment against the four market traits — **Pain, Purchasing power, Easy to target, Growing** ([HORMOZI.md](HORMOZI.md)). A great offer to a bad market loses. If the segment fails, narrow or pivot before going further.

5. **Draft the two ICPs.** Company-level (firmographics + situation) and person-level (role, what they're measured on, what they fear/want). Anchor both on the **Dream Outcome** you uniquely deliver — "we help [who] go from [before] to [after] without [pain]".

6. **Define signals.** Two kinds: **fit signals** (static — right account/person) and **timing signals** (dynamic — *now* is the moment: hiring, funding, job change, new post, tool adoption, expansion). Timing signals are where outreach earns its edge — prioritize them. Map each to a concrete search filter or data query so it's executable, and validate by actually running 1–2 searches.

7. **Find potential customers — prefer the lookalike method.** Don't guess a target list from abstract firmographics if customers exist. **Reverse the best customers into a lookalike search**: take the shared denominators from step 2 (industry, headcount band, region, buyer title/seniority) and run them as a people/company search (Sales Navigator is the reference tool). Eyeball the first page, tighten until the list is mostly on-target, and **record the exact filter set in the brief** so anyone can reproduce it. Fall back to first principles only when there are no usable customers yet. See [SIGNALS.md](SIGNALS.md#lookalike-method-preferred-when-you-have-customers).

8. **Find tribes.** Tribes = shared identities that earn a genuine "we're the same kind of person" opener — alma mater, former employer, hometown/region, community/cohort, conference, sports team, shared investor, niche role identity. Pull real ones from the target profiles. Each tribe must yield a *truthful, specific* ice-breaker — never fabricated commonality.

9. **Interview to fill gaps + align.** Now ask the owner: confirm/correct what you found, fill what data can't (margins, sales motion, deal size, why customers really buy, who churns). Resolve remaining contradictions out loud.

10. **Write the brief.** Produce the markdown deliverable using [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md). If you're in a code repo, save it *outside* the repo (or ask) so it can't get swept into a commit. Every claim traces to data or a stated answer — flag assumptions explicitly.

## Quality bar

- **Specific enough to disqualify.** A good ICP excludes more than it includes. "B2B SaaS founders" is not an ICP; "seed-stage B2B SaaS founders who just raised and posted about hiring their first AE" is.
- **Evidence-backed.** Each firmographic/signal cites where it came from (a search, an enriched profile, or an owner answer).
- **Actionable.** Every signal maps to something detectable in public data. Every tribe maps to a real, honest opener.
- **Honest commonality only.** If a tribe-based ice-breaker would be a stretch or a lie, cut it.
